Casebook · Siddhant Goyal

I go looking for the problem first, then build for it.

I moved from India to Canada to study geomatics, a course that did not even exist back home, at a university nobody I knew had ever been to. My parents took a big risk paying for it. That gap became my first startup: a way for future students to talk to current ones before making the same leap. I built it before talking to enough students, and they would not pay. It taught me the habit I still use: look at what already exists, build a small version, test it with real people, then decide.

At work my teammates call me their personal human AI, or the ideas guy. I am who they come to when something is stuck or taking too long. I like messy operations because there is almost always a way to save time, save money or make money, and with AI I can build the fix myself instead of waiting on a tech team.

I do not see stopped projects as failures. They are paused until a better idea brings them back. And I am less busy than it looks. I have done a full-time master's, a full-time job and university teaching at the same time, while building something on the side, and I still play cricket, volleyball and squash, go to the gym, and fly as a private pilot. I do all of it because I enjoy it.

How I work

  1. Find where people lose the most time or money, usually in a queue of cases nobody has measured.
  2. Measure it before touching it, so the result is real and not a feeling.
  3. Build the smallest thing that removes the manual work, then hand it to the team.
  4. On my own ideas, set a test with a clear pass mark and stop when it fails.

Payment disputes were eating a team's week

Shipped
Flair Airlines · Data Analyst · 2023 to 2025
Problem
A team of 3 to 4 people handled 100+ chargeback cases a week, at about 40 minutes each. Most of that time went into gathering the same evidence again and again.
How I saw it
I timed the work. The judgement part of each case was short. The collecting was the cost.
What I did
Built an AI tool that pulls the evidence for each dispute together and drafts the case, so the analyst reviews and decides.
Result
Each case went from 40 minutes to 5. Over 55 hours a week freed to fight the disputes where the airline was not at fault.

Fuel was burning before the aircraft even moved

Shipped
Flair Airlines · Data Analyst
Problem
Fuel is an airline's biggest controllable cost, and part of it was burned on the ground.
How I saw it
I broke ground fuel into taxi time, auxiliary power unit use and the minutes at the gate before ground power was connected. I calculated burn from both booked and boarded load factors, because no-shows changed the answer.
What I did
Recommended changes to gate and ground procedures on the worst routes.
Result
Helped cut fuel costs by 10% on targeted routes.

Tested, stopped

What I did
Registered the company, cleared the model with the provincial regulator and launched the website. Built a 100-spot budget with break-even at 33 spots, and set a clear test: 5 paid dealer deposits before spending on launch. Got strong dealer interest and the first spot booked.

Do finance influencers' stock picks beat the market?

Built
Finfluencer Scorecard · Python, Claude API, Whisper · 2026
Problem
Millions of people act on stock picks from YouTube, and nobody scores them.
How I did it
When YouTube restricted captions, I switched to a local speech-to-text model. Turned 2,175 videos (about 700 hours of audio) from 10 channels into 474 verified buy and sell calls, with a second model check that rejected nearly half the extractions. Priced every call at the next market open so no result used hindsight. Cut API cost by about 60% along the way.
Result
Two channels looked like winners until I found their picks were correlated. Resampling by stock and by month removed both. No channel measurably beat the market; one trailed it by 10.1% over 3 months.
The interesting result was the one that disappeared under a fair test.

Does sustainability disclosure pay at low-cost airlines?

Research
Research master's thesis · University of Waterloo · 2026
Problem
Airlines publish more sustainability reporting every year. Nobody had tested whether it relates to profit for low-cost carriers.
What I did
Coded 1,289 disclosed practices from the reports of 20 low-cost airlines (2015 to 2024) into an index, then ran panel regressions in R on 128 airline-years.
Result
Overall disclosure had no link to returns. Environmental disclosure was linked to higher return on assets. The negative social link came from airlines in financial distress.
