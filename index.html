About me

Where it started

I moved from India to Canada to study geomatics, a course that did not exist back home, at a university nobody I knew had been to. My parents took a big risk paying for it. That gap became my first startup: a way for future students to talk to current ones. I built it before talking to enough students, and they would not pay.

At work

My teammates call me their personal human AI, or the ideas guy. I am who they come to when something is stuck or taking too long. I like messy operations because there is almost always a way to save time, save money or make money, and with AI I can build the fix myself instead of waiting on a tech team.

Outside work

I do not see stopped projects as failures. They are paused until a better idea brings them back. I have done a full-time master's, a full-time job and university teaching at once while building on the side, and I still play cricket, volleyball and squash, go to the gym, and fly as a private pilot. I do it because I enjoy it.

Travel approvals ran on informal sign-offs

OpenText · Senior Business Analyst · 2025 to now

ProblemTrips in the SAP organization were approved informally, and managers could not see their team's travel budgets before a trip was booked in Concur.

How I saw itInstead of writing a proposal, I built an HTML prototype with Claude to show the fix, took it to the VP, then presented it to multiple senior leaders.

What I didThey approved the build and its budget. I built the app myself, learning the stack on the job (Vue.js, Node.js, PostgreSQL, Docker), with AI policy checks. I wrote the requirements, tested it with users and changed it after each demo.

Now in testing, with employees already using it to track their travel expenses. 70 to 80 employees at launch.
A working prototype gets a yes faster than a slide.
40 → 5 minper disputeShipped

Payment disputes were eating a team's week

Flair Airlines · Data Analyst · 2023 to 2025

ProblemA team of 3 to 4 people handled 100+ chargeback cases a week at about 40 minutes each, mostly gathering the same evidence again and again.

How I saw itI timed the work. The judgement part of each case was short. The collecting was the cost.

What I didBuilt an AI web app that shows each new chargeback and, in one click, pulls everything known about that customer from NAVBLUE, Zendesk, booking and call centre systems into one place to file the dispute. Designed its data schemas so the data engineering team could maintain it, and changed it after each demo with the analysts.

Each case went from 40 minutes to 5, freeing over 55 hours a week to fight the disputes where the airline was not at fault.

−10%fuel costShipped

Fuel was burning before the aircraft moved

Flair Airlines · Data Analyst

How I saw itBroke ground fuel into taxi time, auxiliary power use and gate time before ground power. Calculated burn from booked and boarded load factors, because no-shows changed the answer.

What I didRecommended changes to gate and ground procedures on the worst routes.

Helped cut fuel costs by 10% on targeted routes.
+18%revenue per passengerShipped

Paid extras were underpriced

Flair Airlines · Data Analyst

How I saw itMeasured revenue per passenger, not totals, so passenger growth could not hide weak pricing.

What I didLed pricing analysis and A/B tests on bags, seats and bundles. Measured each test in Google Analytics, checked significance in Optimizely, and read the results through drop-off between booking steps. Also automated financial reconciliation with Power Automate, SQL and Power BI.

Ancillary revenue per passenger up 18%. Reconciliation time down 70%, backlog down 60%.

The Car Bzaar: a one-day used car market

Co-founder · Kitchener-Waterloo · 2026 · thecarbzaar.com

ProblemBuying a used car from a dealer is slow and opaque. One event could put many dealers and buyers in the same room.

What I didRegistered the company, cleared the model with the provincial regulator and launched the site. Built a 100-spot budget with break-even at 33 spots, and set the test: 5 paid dealer deposits before spending on launch. Got strong dealer interest and the first spot booked.

The furthest any venture of mine has gone. Most dealers liked it but would not commit money, so we paused before launch.
Interest is not demand. A deposit is.

Drive: are Ontario drivers overpaying for car insurance?

Founder · Ontario · drive-audit.vercel.app

ProblemCar insurance in Ontario is expensive, and most people never check whether their deductibles, discounts and coverage fit them.

What I didLaunched a free benchmark that shows drivers where they stand, plus a detailed audit: upload your policy declaration page and get a review within 48 hours covering deductibles, coverage gaps, missed discounts, premium competitiveness and telematics. Shared it with one link in one Reddit post.

184 drivers completed the benchmark, and the number is still climbing. 7 uploaded their full policy for an audit. The audits did not find enough savings to make people pay, so I paused before building the monitoring product.
Curiosity got 184 people to answer. Only real savings would get them to pay.

Do finance influencers' stock picks beat the market?

Finfluencer Scorecard · Python, Claude API, Whisper · 2026

How I did itWhen YouTube restricted captions, I switched to local speech-to-text. Turned about 700 hours of audio from 10 channels into verified buy and sell calls, with a second model check that rejected nearly half the extractions. Priced every call at the next market open so nothing used hindsight, and cut API cost by about 60%.

Two channels looked like winners until I found their picks were correlated; resampling removed both. No channel measurably beat the market, and one trailed it by 10.1% over 3 months.
The interesting result was the one that disappeared under a fair test.

Does sustainability disclosure pay at low-cost airlines?

Research master's thesis · University of Waterloo · 2026

What I didCoded disclosed practices from 20 low-cost airlines (2015 to 2024) into an index and ran panel regressions in R on 128 airline-years.

Overall disclosure had no link to returns; environmental disclosure was linked to higher return on assets.
